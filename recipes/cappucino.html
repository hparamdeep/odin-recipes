Cappucino

Description

A cappuccino is a classic Italian espresso-based coffee drink defined by its precise balance of equal parts espresso, steamed milk, and airy microfoam. Named after the brown robes of Capuchin friars, a traditional cappuccino delivers a bold coffee flavor mellowed by the rich, velvet-like texture of aerated milk. Unlike a latte, which contains a higher proportion of steamed milk and a thinner foam layer, a cappuccino features a thick, pillowy crown of foam that insulates the drink, keeping it hot while providing a distinct contrast between warm liquid coffee and creamy froth.

Ingredients

  • 1 shot (or 2 shots) of freshly brewed espresso
  • 4 to 5 oz (120 to 150 ml) fresh whole milk

Steps

  1. Grind fresh coffee beans and extract a single or double shot of espresso directly into a pre-warmed cappuccino cup (approx. 5 to 6 oz capacity).
  2. Pour cold whole milk into a stainless steel frothing pitcher. Submerge the steam wand tip just below the surface to incorporate air (aeration/stretching) until the milk expands by about 50%, then lower the wand slightly to swirl and heat the milk to roughly 60–65°C (140–150°F).
  3. Tap the pitcher firmly on the counter to break any large surface bubbles, then swirl it in circles to blend the liquid milk and microfoam into a glossy, wet-paint texture.
  4. Pour the textured milk directly into the center of the espresso shot, starting high to allow the liquid milk to sink beneath the crema, then bringing the pitcher low near the surface to gently float a thick layer of foam on top.
